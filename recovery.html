Scam recovery

Sent money or shared details? Here's what to do.

You're not alone, and it's not your fault. Scammers are skilled criminals. Work through these steps in order. Step 1 matters most, so do it now.

Your ticks and notes are saved only in this browser, on this device.

Your 5 recovery steps

0 of 5 steps done

  1. 1
    Step 1: stop the loss. Do this now

    Call your bank now

  2. 2
    Step 2

    Report it

    • ReportCyber if you lost money or were hacked online. Your report may be passed to police.
    • Scamwatch to help warn others and stop scammers.
    • Write down any report or reference numbers in your case file below.
    Report to ReportCyber (opens in a new tab) Report to Scamwatch (opens in a new tab)
  3. 3
    Step 3

    Protect your identity

    • Change passwords for your email and banking, starting with your email. Use a device the scammer hasn't touched.
    • Turn on two-step sign-in (a code sent to your phone) wherever you can.
    • If you shared ID documents, contact IDCARE for a free plan. They can help with licences, passports and credit reports.
    • Ask a credit reporting agency for a ban on your credit report, so no one can take out loans in your name.
    IDCARE: 1800 595 160 idcare.org (opens in a new tab)
  4. 4
    Step 4

    Make a complaint to your bank

    • Ask for the bank's complaints team (sometimes called "internal dispute resolution").
    • Put it in writing. Ask them to investigate, try to recover the money and reply in writing.
    • Keep a copy and note the complaint reference number.

    Use our draft letter to your bank

  5. 5
    Step 5

    Take it to AFCA if you need to

    • If you're unhappy with the bank's answer, or it hasn't given you a final answer within 30 days, you can complain to the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA).
    • AFCA is free and independent. You don't need a lawyer.
    AFCA: 1800 931 678 Make a complaint at afca.org.au (opens in a new tab)
Warning

Watch out for recovery scams

After a scam, criminals often get back in touch. They pretend to be police, lawyers, government or "fund recovery" companies who can get your money back, for a fee. This is another scam.

  • Official help is free. ReportCyber, Scamwatch, IDCARE and AFCA never charge you.
  • Never pay an upfront fee, or give anyone access to your device, to get money back.
  • If someone contacts you about your case, hang up and call the organisation using details from its official website.
