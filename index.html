Scam alerts

Common scams in Australia

These scams keep coming back in new forms. Know what they look like so you can spot them.

Text message scam

Unpaid toll or fine

A text says you owe a small toll, fine or delivery fee and gives a link to pay quickly.

What to doDon't tap the link. Check your account by typing the official website address yourself.
Government impersonation

Fake myGov or ATO messages

Messages say you're owed a refund, or your account is locked, and ask you to sign in.

What to doSign in to myGov only by typing my.gov.au yourself or using the official app.
Family impersonation

"Hi Mum, I have a new number"

Someone pretends to be your child or grandchild on a new number, then asks for money urgently.

What to doCall them on the number you already have saved before sending anything.
Phone call scam

"Move your money to a safe account"

A caller says they're from your bank or the police and that your money is at risk.

What to doBanks never ask you to move money to keep it safe. Hang up and call the number on your card.
Investment scam

Crypto and "guaranteed" returns

Ads, new online friends or fake celebrity stories promise big, safe profits from investing.

What to doCheck the company on ASIC's Moneysmart website before investing anything.
Computer scam

"Your computer has a virus"

A pop-up or caller says your device or internet is hacked and asks you to install an app like AnyDesk.

What to doNever let a stranger connect to your device. Close the page or hang up.

Can you spot a scam?

7 warning signs of a scam

  • It rushes you

    "Act now", "within 24 hours", "final notice". Pressure stops you from checking.

  • It asks for unusual payments

    Gift cards, cryptocurrency, money transfer services or a "new" bank account.

  • It asks for codes or passwords

    No real organisation needs your one-time code, PIN or password.

  • It sends a link to log in or pay

    Banks, myGov and the ATO say they won't send you links asking you to sign in.

  • It's too good to be true

    Surprise prizes, refunds, easy jobs or guaranteed investment returns.

  • It asks you to keep it secret

    "Don't tell your bank" or "keep this between us" is a big warning sign.

  • It wants access to your device

    Being asked to install an app such as AnyDesk or TeamViewer so they can "help".

The golden rule: stop, then check. Contact the organisation yourself using a number or website you find, not one they gave you.

Talking to family about scams

  • Talk early, without blame

    Anyone can be scammed. Share your own near-misses so it feels normal to talk about.

  • Agree on a family code word

    Pick a word only your family knows. If someone calls or texts asking for money, ask for the word.

  • Practise the "hang up and call back" habit

    Show them how to find the official number on a card, bill or website and call it themselves.

  • Set up bank protections together

    Ask the bank what protections it offers, such as transaction alerts and daily payment limits.

  • Make it easy to ask

    "Send it to me before you reply" is a simple rule. Save ScamTrail on their phone's home screen.

  • If it has happened, stay calm

    Shame stops people asking for help. Focus on the next step and go through the recovery steps together.

Speaking a language other than English? Ask your family to help you check messages, and remember scammers often pretend to be from immigration or visa services.
